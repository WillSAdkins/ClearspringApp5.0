What changed, and why

The accent follows the church year
Violet for Advent and Lent, gold for Christmas and Easter, green for Ordinary Time, red for Pentecost. It's the colour system your building already uses, it tells someone where they are in the year, and it means the app looks different in March than it does in December without anyone redesigning it. All four pass AA contrast in both themes.
Three typefaces, three jobs
Archivo set tight and heavy for headings — confident rather than crafted. Newsreader for scripture only, because reading a chapter is a different act from tapping a menu and deserves a face built for it. JetBrains Mono for every number: verse markers, streaks, scores. Your current build declares four font variables but loads two, so numerals render in proportional type.
Rows and rules instead of cards
Stacked tinted cards with soft shadows are what makes the current screen feel like every other app. Hairline rules and a numbered column are flatter, denser and read as more deliberate.
Three corner radii, not eight
4px, 12px, and full pills. Your stylesheet currently uses eight different values, which is the sort of thing nobody names but everybody feels.
The risk I'd defend
A colour scheme that changes four times a year is harder to art-direct than a fixed one, and gold in particular is a difficult accent. But a church app that ignores the liturgical calendar is leaving its own best material on the table.
